The phrase *morning routine* can make the start of a day sound like a project.
There are charts to follow, habits to stack, and a version of yourself waiting
somewhere at the end of a very early alarm.

A ritual can be much smaller. It can be a few gestures you already know how to
make: opening a window, filling the kettle, setting a notebook on the table.
Nothing needs to happen in the right order. Their usefulness comes from being
familiar enough to give the day a gentle edge.

## Make room, not rules

The best ritual leaves space for the morning you actually have. On a quiet day,
you might linger over breakfast. On a busy one, the ritual may last only as
long as it takes to take a first sip of tea.

Instead of asking, “Did I do my routine?”, try asking, “What would help this
morning feel a little more like mine?” The answer might be opening the
curtains, watering a plant, or allowing five minutes before the inbox.

## Keep one thing that belongs to you

A workday can begin before we have had a chance to arrive in it. One small,
repeatable gesture—a page of reading, a stretch, the first few lines in a
journal—gives the day a beginning that is not an alert or an obligation.

It does not have to transform anything. A ritual is a threshold, not a test.
Cross it in whatever shape the morning allows.
