There is a particular kind of blank page that feels less like an invitation and
more like a quiet request to get things right. The handwriting should be neat.
The thought should be worth keeping. The date should not be several weeks
behind.

It helps to make the notebook less important.

## Leave a door open

Keep a page for things that are not yet thoughts: a phrase overheard on the
bus, a question you do not know how to answer, the name of a color you want to
remember. A note can be a fragment and still be doing its job.

The page becomes easier to return to when it holds possibility instead of
proof. You are not assembling a perfect record of a life. You are leaving
yourself a small place to think.

## Follow the interesting thread

When an idea keeps coming back, give it a little room. Write the question at
the top of a page. Add a list without sorting it. Draw a small, imperfect map
of where the thought might lead.

Some threads end after three lines. Others turn into something you could not
have planned at the outset. Both outcomes are useful. The notebook is not a
promise to finish; it is a way to keep a conversation open.
