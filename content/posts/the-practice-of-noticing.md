Some mornings arrive already full. Before the kettle has boiled, the mind is
sorting messages, rehearsing conversations, and making a list of things that
have not happened yet. On days like these, the world can feel less like a place
we inhabit and more like a background passing behind us.

One small way back is to notice what is already here.

## Start with what is close

On a short walk, try choosing one thing to notice each time you turn a corner.
Not the most beautiful thing. Not something you should be grateful for. Just
something particular.

The last gold leaf caught on a railing. A shopkeeper propping the door open.
Rain darkening the pavement in a shape that will be gone in an hour. These
details do not ask us to improve them. They only ask to be seen.

That distinction matters. Attention is not another task to do well. It is a way
of meeting the day without demanding that it become extraordinary first.

## Let the detail stay small

There is a temptation to turn every observation into a lesson. A passing
cloud becomes a metaphor; a quiet afternoon must become an argument for a slower
life. Sometimes a cloud is simply a cloud, and the afternoon is simply kind.

Letting a moment remain small is its own kind of respect. The point is not to
collect meaning. It is to make enough room for experience to arrive before we
decide what it means.

## A practice without a checklist

If you would like a place to begin, leave your phone in your pocket for the
first five minutes of a familiar walk. Notice the quality of the light, then
one sound, then something you have passed so often you stopped seeing it.

That is enough. There is no streak to maintain and nothing to catch up on
tomorrow. The practice is simply to return, now and then, to the place where
your feet already are.
